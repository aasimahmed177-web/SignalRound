# Coverage: how much of the web to read, and how often

One knob in Settings picks a rung. Every rung is the same product; the rungs differ in how wide
the discovery net is, how deep each company is read, and how often everything runs. Credits scale
with the rung, from a few thousand a month to millions, and the estimate for the current rung and
the current presets is shown beside the selector before anything runs. The default is Lite.

| | Lite (default) | Standard | Deep | Max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sweep | weekly, week window | daily, day window, plus a weekly week window | twice daily | hourly |
| Search sources | news | news + web | news + web | news + web |
| X accounts read per sweep (the shipped presets list none) | none | the preset's list, up to 2 | up to 5 | up to 10 |
| Watch sites (publisher funding sections: one `scrape` of the section's links, then `scrape` of new URLs) | off | off | daily | hourly |
| Stage-free searches and X announcements (ride with watch sites) | off | off | daily | every third hour; X every quarter hour |
| SEC Form D (EDGAR full-text search, then each filing's `primary_doc.xml`) | off | off | daily | hourly |
| Monitor cadence (if a monitor exists) | daily | daily | four times a day | hourly |
| Corroboration (`search` the company after a new record, extract 3 more articles) | off | on for new records | on for new and updated records | on |
| Enrichment | homepage profile and logo | plus the pricing page | plus corroboration sources | plus weekly re-enrichment |
| Alexandria (YC, FullEnrich, and Apollo records for each verified domain) | off | Y Combinator | plus FullEnrich and Apollo firmographics, Apollo's missing rounds searched as history leads | plus FullEnrich and Apollo firmographics, and the leads |
| Trending (`search` on `site:x.com` for each new round at four checkpoints, Hacker News, an hourly rescore, likes for the labelled rounds' top posts) | off | off | on | on |
| Agent gap-fill on review items | manual (Research button) | manual | automatic for every review item | automatic |
| Agent deep dive (founders, prior rounds, headcount, customers, backers, competitors; checked against its sources before it is saved) | manual from the company page | manual | automatic above $10M | automatic for every company, plus two a tick from the backlog |
| Agent discovery sweep (`agent` searches a window for rounds search missed; priced, not built yet) | off | off | weekly | daily |
| Estimated monthly credits, the default preset (Series A and B) | 3.3k to 3.6k | 28k to 30k | 165k to 265k | 2.0M to 2.6M |
| Estimated monthly credits, all three shipped presets | 10k to 11k | 84k to 91k | 434k to 563k | 3.5M to 4.3M |

A scheduled sweep runs every preset, so a fresh clone spends the all-presets figure; delete the preset files you do not want (`presets/seed.json`, `presets/growth.json`) to spend the default-preset one.

Every rung verifies each new record before it publishes: with a model key set, the reconcile step reads the record against every source together (one Firecrawl credit per page read, about a cent of model time a record); without one, the judge reads the primary page (about 5 credits a record). Publish requires the verifier's confirmation. `judge: false` in the overrides turns verification off, and then records land flagged and never publish.

## How the rung is applied

- `lib/coverage.ts` owns the table above as data: `coverageFor(tier)` returns a plan object; `resolveCoverage({ tier, overrides })` merges a stored setting into a plan; `estimateMonthlyCredits(plan, presets, monitors)` prices it from the rate card in `packages/core/src/firecrawl/credits.ts` with a low and a high per component; `sweepDue(plan, at)` is the clock.
- The tier lives in the `settings` table (`key = "coverage"`, a JSON value with the tier and any per-field overrides). Settings has the selector with the estimate and a breakdown by component; an advanced section exposes each field as an override with a reset back to the tier.
- Vercel cron calls `/api/cron/sweep` hourly; the route reads the plan and returns `{ skipped: "not due", next }` unless this hour is due for the rung (Lite: Monday 06:00 UTC, week window; Standard: 06:00 daily, day window, plus the week window on Monday; Deep: 06:00 and 18:00; Max: every hour). One static schedule, one place that decides. `?force=1` sweeps regardless; the deliveries cron is unchanged.
- The lanes read the plan for the knobs that exist today: the sweep's search step passes the plan's sources (Lite searches news only whatever the preset says) and caps X accounts at `xAccountsMax` by slicing the preset's list, so a preset can be narrower than the rung but not wider; enrichment skips the pricing page when the plan says `homepage`; a monitor created from Presets takes the plan's schedule, and saving a rung with a different monitor cadence reschedules every live monitor (`lib/monitor-schedule.ts`).
- `alexandria` gates the Alexandria pass after enrichment: every company with a verified domain gets its Y Combinator record (a name search narrowed to the hit on that domain, then the company read), and `yc_firmographics` adds the FullEnrich lookup keyed on the domain and Apollo's bulk lookup, ten domains a call, whose funding events the store lacks are queued as history leads and searched by the company's history pass. Apollo is on at exactly the rungs that already buy FullEnrich (Deep and Max), which are also the rungs whose deep dives run the history lane; it needs no terms on a Firecrawl key. FullEnrich is a third-party provider whose terms an org admin must accept once at https://www.firecrawl.dev/app/alexandria/fullenrich; until then its calls come back `terms_required` and the company page tells admins so.
- After persist, both workflows read the plan again: `corroboration` picks which persisted rows get a second reading (new, new and updated, all, or off; ten at a time), `agent.gapFill: "auto"` runs the Research play on every new record still in review, and `agent.deepDive` dives each new record's company without a current checked dossier (above `deepDiveMinUsdMillions` at `threshold`, every company at `all`; one company after another, each its own few steps in `workflows/dossiers.ts`). A checked dossier is current until a round that is not history is recorded after it: the new round outdates it (its total and "why it matters" read from before the round), and the company is dived again. A company with a dossier written before the check is only checked, not dived again; one whose dive failed rests a week, one the check rejected a month. At `all`, each sweep tick then works two more visible companies still without a checked dossier, oldest first, so the store converges on a checked dossier per company. The dives run at the end of the tick, after every preset's rounds are recorded and delivered, so a new round never waits on a dive. A dossier that passes its check starts its company's history pass (`workflows/history.ts`), which looks for each prior round's announcement on its own run. The gating is `lib/intel.ts`; corroboration and gap-fill credits land on the sweep's row as `corroboration_credits` and `agent_credits`, and each dive and check has its own row with its credits (the sweep's row counts the companies it queued as `dossiers_queued`).
- Watch sites and Form D run from the same hourly tick (`lib/lanes.ts` decides: hourly cadence every hour, daily at 06:00 UTC) as `workflows/watch-sites.ts` per preset with `watchSites` and `workflows/form-d.ts` once. Both keep their seen state in the `candidates` table under synthetic monitor ids (`watch:<host>`, `formd`), so a page or filing is extracted once. The Form D window is the last two days on the daily cadence and the last three hours on hourly; its records carry preset slug `form-d`, stage `other`, and the label `Form D (equity)`. The lane's gate (`formDDisposition` in `packages/core/src/edgar/form-d.ts`) keeps a filing only when it reads as a venture round: an operating industry, a corporation, a private issuer, equity rather than options or notes alone, no business combination, no vehicle or property-address name, at least $1M sold and, to a single investor, at least $5M; every skip is counted on the run row as `skipped_<rule>`. The public-issuer check costs no extra EDGAR request: the ticker rides on the search hit, and an amendment's submissions index (already read for its predecessor) carries the tickers and periodic reports. `?lane=watch&force=1` and `?lane=formd&force=1` run one lane by hand.
- `trending` gates the Trending lane: Vercel cron calls `/api/cron/trending` hourly and the route answers `{ skipped }` unless the plan has it on (`?force=1` runs it anyway). It stays off at Lite and Standard because its cost is per new record at four checkpoints of searches each: at Standard that is 10,000 to 15,000 credits a month, a third to a half again on the rung, for a ranking a daily desk does not need. At Deep the searches add about a tenth (11,000 to 16,000), and the showcase's likes add up to 54,000 more in a month when five rounds hold the label all month; at Max the searches are a few percent. Each run's credits are summed from what every call reported (search bills per ten results returned; the X scrape reports its own), not the account balance, so a run sharing the hour with a sweep still measures only itself.
- The daily accuracy check (`/api/cron/accuracy`, 05:00 UTC) runs at every rung and reads no plan: its checks are database reads, and its remedies are capped per run rather than per rung (five profile refreshes, two deep dives, three history passes; [data-quality.md](data-quality.md#the-daily-accuracy-check)), so a day that uses every cap costs about two deep dives and a few searches (around 700 credits at the rate the estimate prices a dive), and a day with nothing new to fix costs nothing. `remedies=0` on the route, or caps of 0, turn them off.
- The agent discovery sweep is plan data with no consumer yet. It is priced in the estimate so the rung's cost is honest before the lane catches up.

## How the estimate is priced

The estimator prices the default preset's eight queries (an X account read is 34 credits, one page, when a preset lists accounts; the shipped presets list none), then the lanes the rung turns on. The table above is priced for one preset; each additional preset (seed and pre-seed, Series C and later) adds its own sweep cost on every tick, its own X account reads, and its own monitor if one exists, and the Settings estimate sums every preset file it finds. The lanes are priced from named assumptions in `lib/coverage.ts`, each with a comment: unique pages are 60 to 75 percent of raw search results (the same share the Presets estimate uses), a day window returns about half a week window's results, a week sweep yields about 25 new records (19 to 24 measured on the shipped presets) and a day of daily sweeping about 10 (20 on the hourly rung, where the second pass buys freshness more than recall), verification is 5 credits a record for the read-back before publish (the judge's one page plus the reconciler's four or so source pages), corroboration is one search plus three pages per record, enrichment is one homepage extraction plus a five-result website search for half the companies and a second candidate homepage for the one in five whose first candidate fails the identity check (plus, for each of the three new investors a record names on average, a 2-credit website search and one 5-credit homepage verdict that also reads the logo, 60 a pass at most and 30 more from the backlog; on Lite a 2-credit search and a 1-credit branding-only logo read, 25 a pass and 10 from the backlog), Alexandria is 7 credits a company for the YC record (a search plus a company read on about a third of them) and 36 with FullEnrich's lookup (5) and Apollo's (30 for each company it matches, a miss free; it matched 21 of 26 in the measurement, so 24 a company), Apollo's leads are 0.7 a company (18 over the same 26, after dropping the rounds the store, the ledger, or the dossier already had) at a prior round's 11 to 30 credits, a monitor check is 130 credits times its cadence, gap-fill is 400 credits on the 10 percent of records that land in review, a deep dive is 300 credits per company, the agent at effort high plus the check's reads (effort medium measured 82 a dive over 68 dives, and high is priced at about three times that until the backfill measures it; 30 percent of records above the threshold, every record on Max), each checked dossier then has its prior rounds looked for as history rounds (0.8 prior rounds a dossier as stored, 11 to 30 credits each: a site map, one search, one to five pages until one states the round, and the read-back), Max's backlog is two such dives a tick, with their history rounds, while visible companies still lack a checked dossier (zero once the backfill has drained it, which is why Max's range is wide), a discovery sweep is 1,500 credits a run, Trending is four checkpoints of two to three `site:x.com` searches a record (3.4 credits a search as measured, 4 at the ceiling) plus one credit for the handle, with review records never followed at the low end, and on top of that, instance-wide, the showcase's X scrape of three posts for each of up to five labelled rounds every six hours (30 credits a post; zero when nothing earns the label, 54,000 a month at the ceiling), a watch site is one credit to read the section (priced as a `map` call; a links-only `scrape` costs the same) plus five per new page, with every preset's sites counted (28 shipped) at about 12 new pages a day each (the daily aggregators give up to 40, the regional desks five to ten), the stage-free searches are 8 credits each (20 results from news and web, 30 to 40 returned) every third hour on Max and once a day on Deep with about 11 unread article URLs a day each to extract (18 shipped), the X announcements lane is twelve queries a run, each a 4-credit `site:x.com` search and a 30-credit search of X's own index through Alexandria (every quarter hour on Max, daily on Deep) plus 15 to 40 new leads a day at three to six 2-credit article searches and one to three pages each, and one to five founder checks a day at a 30-credit bio read and a 1-credit homepage read, and Form D costs no Firecrawl credits. The sweep's seen-state means an hourly day-window sweep extracts only pages it has not read in three days, so the sweep line is now a ceiling. The estimate is what Settings shows; the measured credits per run are on Runs.

A backfill (`pnpm backfill --months N`) is priced per preset per month like one week sweep under the plan: search returns the same number of results per query whatever the window, so a month costs the queries plus the pages they turn up, and the Form D run over the span is free.

## What each rung buys

Lite is a weekly picture of the market for the price of a few hundred pages a week; the first sweep fits in Firecrawl's free plan, and a month of Lite on the default preset fits in Hobby. Standard is a daily desk: every round the news reports, dated and corroborated, with logos and pricing. Deep is a research team: publisher sections and Form D catch what search misses, every review item is researched, every sizable round gets a full company dossier. Max is the firehose: hourly everything and a dossier on every company.
